# ELCEO Visual Language

## Identity

ELCEO should feel serious, editorial, cinematic, spatial, and market-literate. It must not look like a generic AI SaaS, crypto exchange, broker, gaming interface, or neon cyberpunk template.

## Color roles

- **Canvas:** true/near black and deep charcoal.
- **Primary text:** soft white / near white.
- **Core accent:** ELCEO orange, burnt orange, amber, restrained gold.
- **Tension/context accent:** burgundy, claret, wine red.
- **Analytical/transition accent:** selective blue/cobalt/electric-blue family where a scene specifically calls for it.
- **Principle-card chroma:** deliberately varied fields (amber, green, burgundy/red, blue, magenta/pink) only inside the approved editorial card system.

Orange is not a universal glow treatment. Near-black cards and panels may remain quiet, separated through subtle luminance, shadow, image depth, or a restrained neutral outline.

## Surfaces

Use a small hierarchy:

1. canvas / void;
2. recessed near-black surface;
3. raised tonal surface;
4. scene-specific chromatic field.

Avoid default white cards, generic glassmorphism everywhere, or repeated 1px orange borders. Use glass/translucency only when spatial context justifies it.

## Spacing system

Spacing is a design category, not one generic `gap` scale.

### Micro spacing
Inside controls, labels, icon/text pairs, and compact metadata. Tight, functional, consistent.

### Component spacing
Padding and internal rhythm inside cards, dialogs, controls, and application panels.

### Composition spacing
Distance between text blocks, images, cards, and visual anchors inside one scene. This is often intentionally asymmetric.

### Scene spacing
Large voids that give cinematic compositions room. Do not automatically compress these to make the page shorter.

### Transition spacing
Quiet visual territory required for one scene to resolve before the next scene enters. It may be empty black space, fading atmosphere, or a persistent motif rather than a visible divider.

Negative space is part of the composition. Do not fill it with extra copy, badges, decorative icons, or feature lists.

## Grid and alignment

Use grid systems as construction tools, not as visible templates. Approved compositions may intentionally break equal-column alignment. Optical alignment overrides mathematically perfect centering when the visual balance demands it.

Public scenes may use wide/full-bleed visual fields with narrower text measures. Application pages should use a more disciplined operational grid while retaining ELCEO typography, surface hierarchy, and motion logic.

## Typography

### Direction

Use a contemporary grotesk/neo-grotesk display vocabulary with enough personality to function as architecture at large scale. Do not default to a fashionable serif/sans pairing merely to look premium.

Final font family selection must be license-safe for web embedding and production distribution. Prefer open or explicitly licensed web fonts; never copy proprietary reference-site font files.

### Roles

Define semantic tokens such as:

- display-hero
- display-scene
- heading-lg
- heading-md
- body-lg
- body
- body-sm
- label
- caption
- numeric/data

Do not use arbitrary one-off font sizes where a role exists.

### Display rules

- tight line-height, roughly around 1.0–1.1 depending on face and scale;
- slightly negative optical tracking for very large sizes when appropriate;
- deliberate line breaks and balanced wrapping;
- use display text as spatial material, not simply a heading above a card grid;
- avoid every section using the same heading size/placement.

### Reading/UI rules

- body copy generally 16px+ with comfortable 1.5–1.6 line-height;
- smaller UI/captions only where contrast and legibility remain strong;
- cap paragraph measure; do not stretch prose across ultrawide screens;
- dynamic numerical values use tabular numerals;
- mobile inputs remain at least 16px.

## Iconography

Primary ELCEO iconography should be custom or deliberately curated into one visual family. Use commodity icon libraries only for ordinary utility actions where custom work adds no brand value.

Avoid mixing outline styles, corner treatments, stroke weights, and perspective conventions across one screen.

## Imagery

Imagery should support market context, evidence, uncertainty, global relationships, decision discipline, or the product environment. Avoid generic stock traders at laptops, money imagery, luxury clichés, rockets, coins, handshakes, or obvious AI-brain graphics.

When human scale appears, it should support spatial storytelling rather than become lifestyle advertising.

## Editorial composition

Prefer:

- asymmetry with controlled balance;
- object-led scenes;
- large intentional voids;
- visual hierarchy through scale/depth rather than boxes around everything;
- concise copy anchored to meaningful visual objects;
- transitions that preserve motifs across scenes.

Reject:

- repeated `eyebrow + heading + paragraph + 3 cards` templates;
- identical rounded cards across all pages;
- decorative chips with no function;
- random glowing gradients behind every section;
- excessive badges and microcopy used to fill space.

## Public vs application

The landing/public experience may be cinematic and editorial. Authenticated application pages must become more operational and information-dense, but they still inherit ELCEO's typography, dark surface logic, restraint, motion hierarchy, and editorial clarity.

The accepted dashboard remains a major design anchor for the authenticated product and must not be overwritten by public-site aesthetics.
